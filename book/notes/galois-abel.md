### Abel
- Born 5 August 1802; died 6 April 1829 at Froland, of tuberculosis.
  Wikipedia; Britannica; snl.no.
- Father's career ended in disgrace (false charges in the Storting) and
  drinking; died 1820, leaving no money. MacTutor/Wikipedia (via search).
- Bader beat a pupil in November 1817 who died eight days later; Holmboe
  replaced him, set independent problems, spotted Abel; Holmboe got him a
  scholarship and raised money from colleagues for university. abelprize.no
  biography; MacTutor Holmboe page (via search).
- 1821 false "solution" of the quintic; Degen (Copenhagen) doubtful; Abel
  found the error himself. NTNU slides; Degen letter to Hansteen 21 May 1821
  (via search). *The common story that Degen asked for a numerical example
  is not used.*
- 1824 pamphlet printed at his own expense, squeezed onto six pages (half a
  folio sheet) to save money. Britannica; Manchester biography page.
- Crelle and his journal (founded 1826), Abel's work in its early volumes.
- Paris from 10 July 1826. Letter: "Cauchy is mad, and there is no way of
  being on good terms with him, although at present he is the only man who
  knows how mathematics should be treated" (Abel, *Oeuvres* II, 259; via
  todayinsci and others). The text quotes only the end of this sentence and
  paraphrases the start.
- Memoir submitted 30 October 1826; referees Cauchy and Legendre. Legendre
  to Jacobi, 8 April 1829: "the memoir was barely legible; it was written in
  ink almost white, the letters badly formed." Legendre's anonymous report
  says he passed it to Cauchy. Cauchy mislaid it; found after Abel's death,
  published 1841. Libri supervised printing; manuscript vanished; Libri was
  later convicted (in absentia) of library theft. Del Centina, *Historia
  Mathematica* 29 (2002); us.es blog; ing.dk (2002). A portion was found in
  Florence by Viggo Brun (1952) and the remainder later identified by Del
  Centina; this was cut from the text for length.
- Returned to Christiania May 1827, in debt, no post.*
- Christmas 1828 sled journey to Froland, where his fiancée Christine Kemp
  was a governess at the ironworks; coughed blood after a ball. snl.no;
  hvg.hu; abelprize.no.
- Crelle's letter announcing the Berlin appointment dated 8 April 1829, two
  days after Abel died (abelprize.no; German Strick biography: "zwei Tage
  später"). Legendre's letter to Jacobi bears the same date. **The outline's
  "(verify the timing)" checks out.**
- Keilhau, Abel's friend, offered to marry Christine Kemp without having
  met her; married 1830. Wikipedia "Baltazar Mathias Keilhau"; snl.no.
- 1830 Grand Prix awarded posthumously to Abel and jointly to Jacobi.
  Wikipedia (Galois article).
- Vigeland monument, Slottsparken, 1908; Klein described "a youthful athlete
  of the Byronic type ... striding upward, over two atrocious victims."
  lokalhistoriewiki; Cambridge (*Harmony of the World*); vigeland.museum.no.
- Abel Prize proposed by Sophus Lie in 1899; established 2002 (bicentenary).
  Wikipedia; abelprize.no history page. (First awarded 2003; not stated.)

### Galois
- Born 25 October 1811, Bourg-la-Reine; father mayor from 1815. Louis-le-Grand
  from age 12 (1823).
- Teacher reports: Vernier, "mathematics is the passion that dominates him";
  Louis Richard, "works only in the highest realms of mathematics." Berkeley
  Math Circle handout; ugr.es; Gardner (ETSU). Others called him "singular,"
  "bizarre," "closed" (paraphrased as "odd, secretive and unteachable").
- Legendre's geometry "like a novel," in two days: told as legend; text says
  "is said to" and "may be an exaggeration."
- First Polytechnique failure 1828 (aged 16), without the usual preparation.
- 1829 papers to the Academy, Cauchy referee.
- Father's suicide July 1829 after the priest circulated forged, malicious
  epigrams signed with the mayor's name; hanged himself in Paris near
  Louis-le-Grand; protests and stone-throwing at the funeral. MacTutor;
  Wikipedia (via search). *Sources disagree on the date (MacTutor: 2 July;
  Wikipedia: 28 July). The text says only "July 1829."*
- Second Polytechnique failure days or weeks later; examiner Dinet.
- Taton found a Cauchy letter in the Academy archives showing he planned to
  present Galois's work in January 1830; Galois very probably withdrew the
  manuscripts; Cauchy suggested a Grand Prix entry. Gresham/Neumann (via
  search); Taton, *Revue d'histoire des sciences* (1971).*
- Grand Prix entry to Fourier, February 1830; Fourier died 16 May 1830;
  manuscript lost. Wikipedia.
- July 1830: Guigniault locked students in; Galois tried to climb the wall;
  December letter to the *Gazette des Écoles*; expelled; joined the
  Artillery of the National Guard, abolished 31 December 1830. MacTutor;
  Wikipedia.
- 9 May 1831 banquet (about 200 people; Vendanges de Bourgogne), toast with
  glass and open knife; arrested next day; acquitted 15 June. Gardner;
  Dumas, *My Memoirs* vol. VI.
- 14 July 1831 arrest on the Pont Neuf in artillery uniform, with pistols,
  loaded rifle, dagger; convicted 23 October; six months in Sainte-Pélagie.
  Wikipedia.
- Poisson and Lacroix report (memoir submitted 17 January 1831; report about
  4 July 1831): "neither sufficiently clear nor sufficiently developed to
  allow us to judge its rigor"; suggested he publish the whole work. Reached
  Galois in prison in October. Wikipedia; Brechenmacher, "Galois Got his
  Gun" (arXiv 1111.0655).
- Raspail's *Lettres sur les prisons de Paris*: mocked as a water-drinker,
  drank a bottle of brandy; apparent suicide attempt. damninteresting.com;
  Wikipedia; Gallica holds the original. *Hedged ("seems to have").*
- 16 March 1832 transferred to the maison de santé of Faultrier during the
  cholera epidemic; Stéphanie-Félicie Poterin du Motel, a resident
  physician's daughter; two letters torn up and reconstructed. Gardner;
  Wikipedia.
- Opponent: Ernest Duchâtelet is the usual candidate (Livio suggests two
  opponents, Duchâtelet and Faultrier). Golem/Baez "Galois' Fatal Duel";
  Science News. Text: "the likeliest candidate."
- Letter "to all republicans": "I die the victim of an infamous coquette and
  her two dupes" (translations vary, e.g. "a cruel coquette, and of two of
  her victims"). The text quotes only "the victim of an infamous coquette"
  and paraphrases the rest.
- Letter to Chevalier, 29 May 1832: "Ask Jacobi or Gauss publicly to give
  their opinion, not as to the truth, but as to the importance of these
  theorems. Later there will be, I hope, some people who will find it to
  their advantage to decipher all this mess." Standard translation, several
  sources.
- Shot in the abdomen 30 May; taken to the Cochin hospital; died 31 May 1832.
  Last words to Alfred: "Ne pleure pas, Alfred! J'ai besoin de tout mon
  courage pour mourir à vingt ans." Text says "is reported to have told him."
- Funeral 2 June 1832; 2,000 to 3,000 republicans; police surveillance;
  common grave at Montparnasse, location unknown. Lamarque's funeral on
  5 June set off the June Rebellion (the uprising in *Les Misérables*).
  MacTutor "Messages" page; Wikipedia.
- Liouville announced the work to the Academy in 1843 and published it in
  his *Journal* in 1846.
- Weyl, *Symmetry* (1952): "This letter, if judged by the novelty and
  profundity of ideas it contains, is perhaps the most substantial piece of
  writing in the whole literature of mankind." The text quotes the second
  half and paraphrases the first.

### The legend versus the record
- Bell, *Men of Mathematics* (1937), chapter "Genius and Stupidity": "All
  night he had spent the fleeting hours feverishly dashing off his last will
  and testament, writing against time to glean a few of the great things in
  his teeming mind before the death which he foresaw could overtake him.
  Time after time he broke off to scribble in the margin 'I have not time; I
  have not time,' and passed on to the next frantically scrawled outline."
  Science News, "The Galois Story"; Corry (tau.ac.il).
- Rothman, "Genius and Biographers: The Fictionalization of Evariste Galois,"
  *American Mathematical Monthly* 89:2 (Feb. 1982), 84–106.
- The note appears once: "Il y a quelque chose à compléter dans cette
  démonstration. Je n'ai pas le temps," in the left margin beside the proof
  of Proposition II of the memoir (the January 1831 memoir returned after
  Poisson's report, which Galois annotated on 29 May 1832). arXiv 2607.20147
  citing Neumann (2011). Science News places it "in a final letter ... next
  to a particular theorem," which seems to be a looser description; the text
  follows Neumann. "Sixteen months earlier" = January 1831 to May 1832.

### The mathematics
- Mutilated chessboard: a standard impossibility argument (invariant).
- Galois groups: a general quintic's group is S5 (120 elements); a formula in
  radicals requires a solvable group. S4 (24) has the chain 24 → 12 → 4 →
  1 with abelian steps; S5 → A5 (60), and A5 is simple and non-abelian. The
  prose ("unpicking these shuffles in stages, each of a particularly simple
  and orderly kind") is a deliberate simplification of a composition series
  with abelian factors.
- x⁵ − x − 1: irreducible (Artin–Schreier mod 5), its factorization mod 2 is
  (x² + x + 1)(x³ + x² + 1), so the group has a 5-cycle and a transposition,
  hence is S5; not solvable by radicals. One real root, ≈ 1.1673 (checked by
  hand: f′ vanishes at ±0.669 and the local maximum is negative).
- x⁵ = 2 is solvable (fifth root of 2).
- "Group": Galois is usually credited with the first use in something like
  its technical sense. Hedged with "usually credited."

## Outline claims that turned out wrong or overstated
- **"Manuscripts were lost by Cauchy."** Taton's archival find shows Cauchy
  planned to present Galois's work in January 1830; Galois probably withdrew
  it himself. Cauchy did mislay *Abel's* 1826 memoir. The chapter makes this
  irony part of the story.
- **"Fourier, who died holding one."** Embellished. Fourier took the Grand
  Prix entry home and died on 16 May 1830; it was never found among his
  papers. Nothing says he died holding it.
- **"Galois twice failed the École Polytechnique entrance exam"**: correct.
- **Bell's frantic night**: the outline is right; the papers predated the
  night, and the note appears once.
- **Abel's Berlin letter**: confirmed. Crelle's letter is dated 8 April 1829,
  two days after Abel's death.
